Mahatma Gandhi (1869–1948), fondly known as Bapu and the "Father of the Nation," was a pioneering leader of India's independence movement. Through his philosophies of non-violence (Ahimsa) and truth (Satyagraha), he led peaceful, nationwide movements against British rule. His commitment to simple living, self-reliance, and equality continues to inspire global peace movements. Early Life and Education Born on October 2, 1869, in Porbandar, Gujarat, Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was raised in a traditional Hindu household. After his early education in India, he traveled to London to study law. His experiences with racial discrimination in South Africa, where he worked as a lawyer, transformed him into a dedicated civil rights activist. Philosophy and Principles Gandhiji’s life was guided by two core principles: Ahimsa (Non-violence): He believed that true strength lay in non-violent resistance rather than physical force. Satyagraha (Truth-force): He insisted on truth and ethical, peaceful methods to oppose injustice. He championed social reforms, including the eradication of untouchability, women's empowerment, and the promotion of religious harmony. Role in India's Freedom Struggle Upon returning to India in 1915, he joined the Indian National Congress and assumed leadership of the freedom movement.
